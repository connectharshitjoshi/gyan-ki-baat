☕ Gyaan Ki Baat #9 — What Happens When You Pay via Credit Card on Amazon or Zomato? 💳🍔

You enter your CVV, submit an OTP, and within 3 seconds your order is confirmed. 
What actually happens under the hood across Visa, Mastercard, Razorpay, and Bank servers? 🏦👇

Here is the 2-minute fintech breakdown every engineer should know:

---

1️⃣ Why Merchants Never Store Your 16-Digit Card Number 🛡️
• Under RBI & PCI-DSS rules, Zomato/Amazon cannot save your card PAN or CVV.
• Instead, they use Card Tokenization: The card network generates a unique encrypted device Token. If the merchant DB is hacked, tokens are useless to attackers!

2️⃣ 3D Secure 2.0 (The Anti-Fraud Engine) 🧠
• 3DS2 scores transaction risk in real-time.
• Low risk (small order on home WiFi) ➔ Frictionless flow (instant approval).
• High risk (large amount from unknown device) ➔ Challenge flow (OTP required).

3️⃣ Webhook Idempotency (Why You Aren't Double-Charged) 🔄
• If your internet drops right after submitting OTP, the app retries.
• The server sends a unique `Idempotency-Key`. The Payment Gateway recognizes the retry and returns the existing receipt instead of billing you twice!

4️⃣ Authorization vs. Settlement ($T+1$/$T+2$) ⏳
• Authorization (Instant): Bank holds your credit limit and issues an Auth Code.
• Settlement (24-48 hrs): At night, batched funds clear across Visa/Mastercard into the merchant's bank account.

---

💡 Gyaan Ki Baat:
In distributed payments, network drops are a guarantee. Always design your payment APIs to be idempotent using unique transaction UUIDs! ⚡
