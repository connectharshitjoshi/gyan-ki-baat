☕ Gyaan Ki Baat #8 — How Instagram Handles 100M+ Concurrent Users Without Crashing 🚀📱

When Cristiano Ronaldo posts a photo to 600M followers, or when 50 million people open Instagram during the World Cup, why doesn't the database melt down? 🤯👇

Here is the 2-minute System Design breakdown behind scaling social media apps:

---

1️⃣ The "Celebrity Problem" (Fan-out on Write vs. Read) 🌟
• Fan-out on Write (Push): When a regular user posts, background workers push the post ID into all followers' Redis inboxes. (Instant feed loading $O(1)$).
• The Flaw: If a celebrity with 600M followers posts, you can't run 600 million database writes simultaneously!
• The Fix (Hybrid Model): Celebrities use Fan-out on Read (Pull). Their posts are fetched and merged into your timeline only when you open the app.

2️⃣ Database Sharding by User ID 🗄️
• You can't store 100 billion posts on a single SQL server.
• Instagram shards data across thousands of PostgreSQL/Cassandra nodes using `MurmurHash(userId) % TotalShards`.
• All posts, likes, and profile data for a single user live on the same shard, eliminating expensive cross-server joins!

3️⃣ Global CDN Edge Caching ⚡
• 95% of video and high-res image bandwidth is offloaded to Point-of-Presence (POP) edge servers located close to your city.

4️⃣ Circuit Breakers & Graceful Degradation 🛡️
• If the comments or view-count microservice gets overwhelmed, the system temporarily serves cached metrics rather than crashing your feed.

---

💡 Gyaan Ki Baat:
At scale, you don't query the database to build a user's feed. You serve pre-computed feeds straight out of Redis RAM in sub-5ms! 🧠
